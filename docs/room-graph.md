# Room graph

Generated by `tools/New-RoomGraph.ps1` from the door records in the disc's own
RDT room files. It records structure - room connectivity, exit geometry and
destination references - and no disc bytes, like `disc-manifest.json`.

Totals: 49 rooms, 93 door records, 10 doors whose destination room is not on the
disc, 8 rooms with no door table, 27 rooms reachable from 1:17.

## Driving to a door: how yaw maps to world movement

Measured, not assumed. Holding Up for a known number of frames from a known
position, at three headings 135 degrees apart (each `right:17` turns exactly
1536 yaw units):

| yaw held | measured (dx, dz) normalised |
|---|---|
| 168 | (+0.964, -0.267) |
| 1192 | (-0.258, -0.966) |
| 2728 | (-0.512, +0.859) |

All three fit the same law, so walking to a rectangle is arithmetic rather than
trial and error:

    direction = ( cos(yaw * 2*pi/4096), -sin(yaw * 2*pi/4096) )

Forward speed while holding Up measured ~73 units per frame. Yaw is masked to
0xFFF, so it wraps at 4096, and a heading that would need a negative yaw is
reached by turning the other way.

This is a measurement from one room with railings, so a collision clamps the
displacement and the speed figure is a floor rather than a constant. The
direction law is the part the three headings establish, and the three agree to
within a few thousandths.
